Reversible

Questions for Discussion

For book clubs and reading groups. Use as many or as few as you like.

These questions give away the ending. Finish the book first.

  1. Andrew and Hope have been married forty-one years. What did the swap reveal about their marriage that forty-one years together had not?
  2. Luke is Hope’s mind in a young man’s body; Vivienne is Andrew’s mind in a young woman’s body. When does the book ask you to see them as who they are, and when as who they were—and where did you find that hardest?
  3. The Fosters’ children and grandchildren have to decide whether these two young strangers are their parents. What convinced them? What would it take to convince you?
  4. Andrew’s definition of married love is “the unconditional commitment to the best interest of the other.” Where do you see Luke and Vivienne living that out toward each other after the swap, and where does it cost them most?
  5. Luke applies that same definition to Marcus, a man they have no history with. What made Marcus’s trustworthiness believable—or not—to you?
  6. In The Second Counsel, Thomas answers the Fosters’ question with one of his own: “Did your vows change?” What do you make of where he begins—and of where he draws the line?
  7. Where did you see the Fosters’ faith at work in a decision? Was there a moment you expected it to matter and it didn’t, or the reverse?
  8. If you woke up in your spouse’s body for seven days, what would you most want to understand—and what would you most fear they would learn about you?
  9. The book is called Reversible. By the end, what in the story turned out to be reversible, and what did not?
